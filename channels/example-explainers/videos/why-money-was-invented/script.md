## Hook (title)

What if money was invented just to fix one deeply annoying problem?

[VISUAL: title — "Why Money Was Invented" | "A 90-second explainer"]

## The problem (text)

Before money there was barter. And barter only works if the other person happens to want exactly what you have. Economists call it the double coincidence of wants.

[VISUAL: text — "The double coincidence of wants" | "Both traders must want what the other has."]

## Why it fails (text)

You have eggs and you want shoes. But the shoemaker doesn't want eggs. Now you're chasing a three-way trade. As towns grew and people specialized, that got nearly impossible.

[VISUAL: text — "Barter doesn't scale" | "Specialization made matches too rare."]

## The fix (text)

So people started accepting one thing almost everyone wanted: salt, grain, metal. A universally wanted good let you split a trade in two. Sell now, buy later.

[VISUAL: text — "A universally-wanted good" | "Sell now, buy later."]

## First coins (image)

Around 630 BCE, the kingdom of Lydia stamped the first standardized coins out of electrum, and the idea spread across the ancient world.

[VISUAL: image — "assets/example-explainers/why-money-was-invented/lydian-coin.jpg" | "Lydian electrum coin — public domain"]

## Payoff (text)

So money isn't really about gold or paper. It's a shared agreement that turns one impossible trade into two easy ones.

[VISUAL: text — "Money is an agreement" | "One impossible trade becomes two easy ones."]
